Selected contributions

Research depth.
Technical delivery. Shared progress.

Computational neuroscience

Connecting white matter to brain function

I develop methods to study how structural connections relate to functional brain networks, building on my work in cognitive control, episodic memory, and mental health.

My contribution: Development of a white-matter termination atlas and functional-decoding framework integrating anatomical and diffusion MRI data from 1,062 Human Connectome Project participants.

Read the preprint ↗

Reproducible research infrastructure

Turning complex data into usable research

I develop reproducible neuroimaging workflows using Docker and Singularity across local computing resources and the Texas Advanced Computing Center.

My contribution: Trained more than a dozen undergraduate researchers in Docker and fMRIPrep, and helped secure approximately 900 TB of storage through the AWS Open Data Sponsorship Program for brainlife.io and APEX.

Explore the brainlife.io paper ↗

Research programs & data governance

Coordinating science across teams

As APEX Data Coordinating Center Project Manager, I coordinate work across projects producing multimodal, multiscale, and multispecies brain-connectivity data.

My contribution: Support for data harmonization, milestone tracking, repository coordination, and scientific meetings, alongside international data-governance work through BRIDGE and standards development through BIDS.

Teaching & mentorship

Making rigorous science
accessible to learners.

I help students build confidence in asking questions, evaluating evidence, and making defensible analytical decisions. My approach combines active learning, clear expectations, practical research tasks, and constructive feedback.

I mentor undergraduate and graduate researchers across disciplines, supporting study design, reproducible analysis, scientific writing, and conference presentations.

Statistics & research methods

Laboratory instructor for UT Austin’s PSY 420M in 2021, leading a virtual section with applied data analysis, small-group problem-solving, feedback, and individual support.

Workshops & scientific training

Guest lectures and international workshops in neuroimaging, functional connectivity, meta-analysis, reproducible computing, and open science.

Mentorship & critical thinking

Led laboratory meetings for approximately 15–20 students and supervised projects that progressed to conference presentations and manuscripts. My teaching philosophy emphasizes transparent reasoning, responsible AI use, and evaluation of primary evidence.

03 / Background

A foundation in physics.
A focus on the brain.

My training spans physics, human neuroimaging, and cognitive neuroscience. I earned my bachelor’s degree in physics at Texas Lutheran University and my PhD in Radiological Sciences, with an emphasis on human neuroimaging, at the University of Texas Health Science Center at San Antonio.

I completed postdoctoral training at UC Davis with Cameron Carter (2014–2017) and at UT Austin with David Schnyer (2017–2019). Since 2019, I have served as a Research Assistant Professor at UT Austin, connecting independent research with scientific infrastructure, collaborative program management, and education.
